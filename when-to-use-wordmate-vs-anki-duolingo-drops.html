Wordmate · Is it right for you?

When is Wordmate the right vocabulary app?

Wordmate is a good fit if you want practical, topic-based vocabulary (airport, hotel, asking directions and more) in any of 44 languages, would rather pay once than subscribe, and like to review in spare minutes from a Home Screen widget or Apple Watch. It is not the best fit if you want full grammar or conversation courses, want to build flashcard decks from your own material, or want to start without paying anything up front.

Written by the developer of Wordmate. We tried to keep this fair: the table below only lists what each app's own store listing says.

Wordmate works well if you…

  • Need everyday words for a trip, a move or a new job: 84 units in 14 practical topics, at three levels.
  • Study more than one language. Each language keeps its own progress, and you choose the interface language and the learning language separately.
  • Prefer one purchase that includes every language and feature, with no subscription and no in-app purchases.
  • Learn in short bursts. On iPhone you can view a word, move to the next one or save it right from the interactive Home Screen widget; on Apple Watch you can learn, hear the system voice, save favorites and check your progress.
  • Want privacy: no account required, no third-party ads, no tracking or analytics.

Another app may suit you better if you…

  • Want structured lessons in grammar, speaking and writing. Wordmate is built for vocabulary; a full-course app like Duolingo covers more skills.
  • Want to make your own cards from class notes or textbooks. Anki is designed around decks you create or import.
  • Want to begin at no cost. Duolingo and Drops are free to download.

Wordmate vs Anki, Duolingo and Drops: the facts

Based on their App Store listings: Wordmate is one purchase with no subscription and no in-app purchases, covers 44 languages, and has an interactive Home Screen widget and an Apple Watch app on iPhone. AnkiMobile is paid up front with no subscription and syncs with Anki on Mac, Windows, Linux and the web. Duolingo is free to download with an optional Super Duolingo subscription and lists 40+ languages. Drops is free to download with an optional subscription and lists 55+ languages.

Want full sentences too? The Wordmate & DailyMate Bundle

Wordmate is for words. DailyMate, a separate paid app from the same developer, is for complete everyday phrases: 8,400 phrases in 47 learning languages, taught as full sentences along with the natural replies that follow. The App Store sells the two together as the Wordmate & DailyMate Bundle, priced lower than buying both apps separately. Neither app has a subscription. If you already own one of them, the App Store's Complete My Bundle takes what you paid for it off the bundle price.
